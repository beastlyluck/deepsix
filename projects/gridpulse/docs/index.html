GridPulse

A digital twin for a 48-bus regional microgrid: wind on the ridge, solar on the plain, three battery sites, thirty load centres and a grid tie. The twin answers one operational question each morning — which lines can we actually run at their nameplate today, and what does the N-2 we fear most do to the evening peak under that schedule?

Physical assetDecentralised regional microgrid, 48 buses, 112 lines, 1 Hz SCADA
Final productAutomated load-balancing engine: PINN dynamic line ratings → HiGHS LP dispatch → SGC cascade-risk scorer on a Kafka/Flink feature stream
Technical bottleneckPhysics-informed thermal limits on conductors; graph-structured prediction of cascading failure

Power flow and the cascade model

DC approximation: \( P = B\theta \), \( f = \mathrm{diag}(1/x)\,A\,\theta \), where \( A \) is the line–bus incidence matrix and \( x \) per-unit reactance. The system is solved per connected island with one reference bus each so a tripped tie can never produce a singular matrix. Islands without the grid tie are balanced pro rata before solving.

Cascade: lines trip when \( |f_\ell| > 1.12\, \bar f_\ell \) (emergency rating above the continuous one). The grid is re-islanded and re-solved until nothing else trips or twelve rounds pass. A load bus is lost if it ends up in an island whose generation covers less than 70 percent of its demand.

Ratings are set from the conductor, not from the base case: \( \bar f_\ell = \max(1.35\,|f^{\text{base}}_\ell|,\ 6\ \text{MW}) \). Six lines on busy corridors are deliberately tight at 1.08× base flow — the weak links that give a cascade somewhere to start.

PTDF: \( \partial f / \partial P \) is computed column by column from unit injections against the slack; the dispatch LP uses it to make line flows linear in the decision variables.

PINN: conductor temperature along a span

A transmission line's continuous rating assumes uniform convective cooling. Real spans have a sheltered section — a cutting, a tree line — where wind cooling drops and a hotspot forms well before the conductor as a whole is at its limit. The PINN learns \( T(x,t,I) \) for the whole loading range at once, so the dispatcher can ask "how long can I run this line at 92 percent?" without a solver in the loop.

Non-dimensional governing equation on \( x\in[0,1] \), \( t\in[0,1] \) (one hour), \( I \) in per-unit of the static rating:

\[ \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = a\,\frac{\partial^2 T}{\partial x^2} + Q\,I^2 - b(x)\,\bigl(T - T_{\text{amb}}\bigr), \qquad b(x) = 5 - 3.2\,e^{-((x-0.55)/0.12)^2} \]

with \( a = 0.02 \), \( Q = 256 \), \( T_{\text{amb}} = 25\ ^\circ\text{C} \), limit \( T_{\text{lim}} = 90\ ^\circ\text{C} \).

Boundary conditions \( T(0,t) = T(1,t) = T_{\text{amb}} \) (towers act as heat sinks) and initial condition \( T(x,0) = T_{\text{amb}} \) are enforced exactly by construction rather than penalised.

Loss function and boundary constraints

Ansatz in the scaled variable \( \theta = (T - T_{\text{amb}})/60 \):

\[ \theta_\phi(x,t,I) = 4\,x(1-x)\,\bigl(1 - e^{-5t}\bigr)\; f_\phi(x,t,I) \]

\( x(1-x) \) vanishes on both boundaries; \( 1 - e^{-5t} \) vanishes at \( t=0 \) and hands the network a first-order rise for free. \( f_\phi \) is a 3×64 tanh MLP.

Residual, \( O(1) \) by construction:

\[ r_\phi = \partial_t \theta_\phi - a\,\partial_{xx}\theta_\phi - \tfrac{Q}{60} I^2 + b(x)\,\theta_\phi \] \[ \mathcal{L}(\phi) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N} r_\phi(x_n, t_n, I_n)^2, \qquad (x_n,t_n,I_n) \sim \text{collocation} \]

Collocation: \( t \sim U(0,1) \), \( I \sim U(0.4,1.4) \), and \( x \) is two-thirds uniform, one-third \( \mathcal{N}(0.55, 0.15) \) so the sheltered span where the second derivative is largest is over-sampled. Second derivatives come from two passes of torch.autograd.grad with create_graph=True. Adam, cosine schedule, 3000 steps of 1536 points; there is no data term.

Verification, not self-report

The PINN is judged against an explicit finite-difference solution (101 points, 4000 steps, \( a\,\Delta t/\Delta x^2 = 0.08 < 0.5 \)) at three loadings. Reported: relative \( L_2 \) of \( T - T_{\text{amb}} \) at \( t=1 \) and the hotspot temperature error. The residual alone is not evidence; a network can have small residual and wrong boundary behaviour.

Boundary statement for the rating

Sustainable current \( I^\star = \sup\{ I : \max_x T(x,1,I) < T_{\text{lim}} \} \), found by bisection on the trained network. With the parameters above \( I^\star \approx 0.86 \): a line that the static rating calls fine at 90 percent will cross 90 °C in the sheltered span within about 40 minutes. The dispatch LP caps any line the static schedule would run above \( I^\star \) at \( I^\star\,\bar f_\ell \) for that hour.

Graph model: cascade risk per bus

Message passing without learned propagation weights. With \( \tilde A = A + I \), \( \tilde D = \mathrm{diag}(\tilde A \mathbf 1) \), the propagator is \( S = \tilde D^{-1/2}\tilde A\,\tilde D^{-1/2} \). Node features \( X \) (net injection, |injection|, live degree, sum and max of incident-line loading, adjacency to the tripped line, count of hot lines) are propagated and hop-concatenated:

\[ H_K = \bigl[\,X \;\|\; SX \;\|\; S^2X \;\|\; \dots \;\|\; S^KX\,\bigr], \qquad \hat p = \sigma\bigl(W H_K + c\bigr) \]

Hop concatenation rather than pure \( S^K X \): a bus's own loading matters as much as its neighbourhood's, and plain smoothing dilutes the local features. The readout is a standardised logistic regression with balanced class weights. \( K = 0 \) is the exact ablation — same features, no graph. Labels come from the cascade simulator on 400 sampled contingencies (70 percent N-1, 30 percent N-2, demand scaled 0.9–1.35); the split is by scenario so no outage appears in both train and test.

Edge criticality, used for edge width on the dashboard, is the fraction of cascades in which a line tripped without being the initiating contingency.

Day-ahead dispatch LP

One linear program over 24 hours, solved with HiGHS through scipy.optimize.linprog. Per hour: renewable dispatch \( g \le \text{avail} \), battery charge/discharge \( \le 10 \) MW, state of charge \( \le 40 \) MWh, load shed per bus, grid-tie import and export.

\[ \min \sum_h \Bigl( 80\,\text{imp}_h - 20\,\text{exp}_h - 50\,\textstyle\sum g_h + 5\,\textstyle\sum(\text{ch}_h + \text{dis}_h) + 1000\,\textstyle\sum \text{shed}_h \Bigr) \] \[ \text{s.t.}\quad \textstyle\sum g_h + \sum \text{dis}_h - \sum \text{ch}_h + \sum \text{shed}_h + \text{imp}_h - \text{exp}_h = D_h \] \[ \text{soc}_{h} = \text{soc}_{h-1} + \eta\,\text{ch}_h - \text{dis}_h/\eta, \qquad \bigl|\,\Phi\, p_h\,\bigr| \le \bar f_h \]

\( \Phi \) is the PTDF matrix and \( p_h \) the vector of bus injections implied by the decision variables; \( \bar f_h \) is per line per hour and is where the PINN's derating enters. The engine solves the LP twice — static ratings, then derated — and reports the cost of the safer schedule. In the shipped run that is roughly \$10k for a day on which the worst N-2 goes from 30 buses lost to none.

Streaming layer and feature store

stream/features.sql is the Flink job: 60-second tumbling windows per bus, joined to the per-line flow stream through the incident-line view, producing the exact seven features the graph model was trained on. stream/feature_store.py is the sink — DuckDB when available, sqlite otherwise, same DDL. stream/replay.py is the same loop run offline over a dispatched day at ten-minute resolution so the dashboard has something to scrub through; the N-2 contingency is injected at 18:00.

Alerts are emitted on the rising edge only: a bus enters the alert stream when its risk crosses 0.5 and is not re-emitted every window while it stays there.

Drift handling and limits

  • Thermal drift. Ambient temperature and wind change the cooling profile \( b(x) \) hourly. The PINN takes fixed coefficients; production would retrain daily on the forecast or condition on \( T_{\text{amb}} \) and wind as extra inputs. Retrain trigger: FD verification error above 3 percent.
  • Topology drift. Switching changes \( A \). The SGC propagator is recomputed from the live line status; the readout weights are not topology-specific, so no retrain is needed for a reconfiguration. Adding a bus does need one.
  • Label drift. Cascade labels come from the simulator, not from history. Real outages are rare and the model will be miscalibrated on them; the AUC here is against the simulator's notion of loss.
  • Not modelled. Reactive power and voltage collapse, protection timing, generator ramp limits, frequency response of islands. Each of these can make a real cascade worse or better than the DC model says.
